# Kailia's Story — Project Guide

## What this app is
Kailia's Story is a developmental assessment app disguised as an adventure game. Parents or kids complete quests with Kailia, and the app quietly measures performance to estimate a child's developmental age across six domains: fine motor skills, communication, math, sensory, reading, and processing.

**Core design rule: it must never feel like homework or a test.** The child experiences a story and quests. All measurement happens invisibly in the background.

## Characters
Two characters appear throughout the whole adventure — every land, every quest, every screen:

- **Kailia** — the main character and the child's avatar on the adventure. A chibi-style girl, about 6 years old, with short brown hair and bangs. Brave, curious, and kind. She travels through the world map, and the child helps her complete quests.
- **Noel** — a giant panda, Kailia's companion and adventure partner. Warm, gentle, a little goofy. Noel is the "guide" character: he explains every quest in short simple words, gives hints when the child struggles, celebrates every attempt with big reactions, and NEVER says anything is wrong — a miss is always "let's try again together!" Noel also appears in parent-assist moments.

**Art today:** Kailia and Noel use the real artwork in `public/characters/{kailia,noel}/` (shown in its brush-painted version, see "Art & motion style" below). The code-based SVG sprites remain as a fallback for any expression with no image.
- All character art/code lives in one folder: `components/characters/`
- All swap-in logic lives in one config file: `components/characters/characters.config.ts`

**How to swap in real artwork later (plain English, no coding needed beyond dropping files in):**
1. Export final character images (PNG or SVG, transparent background works best) for whichever character/expression you're replacing — e.g. `kailia-happy.png`, `noel-excited.png`.
2. Drop those image files into the `public/characters/` folder.
3. Open `components/characters/characters.config.ts` and add one line pointing that character/expression at your file's path (there's a comment in the file showing exactly how — it's just uncommenting/filling in a line).
4. Save. The app automatically uses your image instead of the placeholder drawing for that expression — nothing else needs to change.

Any expression you don't fill in keeps using the placeholder drawing, so you can swap art in gradually, one image at a time.

## Art & motion style (hand-painted, bouncy)
- **Painted characters.** Every Kailia/Noel picture is shown from `public/characters/painted/`, a brush-stroke repaint of the original art made by `scripts/paint-art.py` (needs Python 3 with Pillow + numpy). `PAINTED_ART` in `characters.config.ts` flips the whole app back to the originals. When new character art is added, repaint it: `python3 scripts/paint-art.py public/characters/kailia/new.png public/characters/painted/kailia/new.png`.
- **Painted drawings.** `components/art/PaintKit.tsx` (mounted once in `app/layout.tsx`) defines shared SVG brush filters: `kp-brush` (static), `kp-boil` (edges re-wobble 8×/s like hand-drawn animation), `kp-edge`. New SVG illustrations wrap their contents in `<g filter="url(#kp-brush)">` or `kp-boil`, and use the warm brown outline `#3b2416`, not black. Every child-facing screen gets the `.kp-paper` grain overlay from `AssessmentGate`.
- **Phone speed (Kelvin, 2026-11-26: "everything is laggy, even the pet voices").** No `backdrop-filter` blur anywhere: over the animated sky it made iPhones stutter. Dim pop-up backdrops with a more solid color instead. On an upright phone the map keeps its own height (`[data-map-main]` in `app/globals.css`) and the page scrolls a little; never let bigger text or new buttons squeeze it.
- **Motion.** Idle loops (`float`, `wiggle`, `sparkle`, `critter-bob`) are animated "on twos" (12 fps, held frames) with squash & stretch; entrances (`bounce-in`, `drop-in`, `pop`, `star-burst`) overshoot and settle. Extra moves: `.kp-hop`, `.kp-jelly`. A miss uses `.shake`, which is a friendly jelly wobble, never a "no" head-shake. To retune the 12 fps loops, edit `scripts/motion-keyframes.mjs` and paste its output into `app/globals.css`. Everything respects Reduce motion.
- **Live characters.** `components/characters/LiveCharacter.tsx` makes the Kailia/Noel portraits move like game characters: breathing, blinking eyelids (eye positions measured on `portrait-front.png`, update them if that art changes), a hop loop for excited/celebrating, and a squish when tapped.
- **Grip pictures.** The pencil-grip step (`app/assessment/games/page.tsx`) shows Kelvin's own cartoon hand pictures, one per grip, in `public/grasp/{palmar,digital_pronate,quadrupod,immature_tripod,dynamic_tripod}.webp` (his originals, title text cropped off; use them as they are, never redraw them). Code-drawn hands were tried five ways and Kelvin found every one scary, so don't draw hands in code. A code-drawn cartoon glove was also tried (2026-11-22, "super ugly"). The "Tap to start!" screen (`components/TapToStart.tsx`) uses Kelvin's own pointing-hand picture, `public/pointer/hand.webp`.

## The adventure structure
The child travels with Kailia through a world map. Each region assesses one domain:

1. **Firefly Forest (fine motor)** — trace glowing paths with finger/mouse, tap moving fireflies, drag stones to build a bridge. Measure: tracing deviation from path, tap accuracy on moving targets, drag steadiness, completion time.
2. **Dragon's Treasure Cave (math)** — count gems to pay the dragon's toll, share berries equally among baby dragons, continue stepping-stone patterns. Measure: accuracy, highest difficulty reached, response time.
3. **Whispering Signposts (reading)** — match words to pictures to reveal paths, find rhyming spell words, read short signs and choose the correct fork. Measure: accuracy by reading level, decoding speed.
4. **Village of the Story Keepers (communication)** — retell the previous chapter, describe a picture to unlock a door, choose what a character should say next. Includes a parent-assist mode where the parent rates verbal responses with simple taps (clear sentence / a few words / gestures only, etc.).
5. **Echo Caves (sensory)** — match sounds to creatures, spot the crystal that differs in color or texture, find the hidden creature in a scene. Measure: auditory and visual discrimination accuracy.
6. **Owl's Tower (processing)** — memory riddles ("which door did the owl fly through?"), follow the owl's multi-step instructions, quickly sort falling potion bottles. Measure: memory span, instruction-following steps completed, sorting speed and accuracy.

Kailia and Noel travel together through every one of these lands — Kailia attempts the quests, Noel introduces them and reacts to every attempt.

## Measurement & adaptivity rules
- Every mini-quest logs: domain, task id, difficulty level, accuracy, attempts, response time, and (where relevant) precision metrics like tracing deviation.
- Tasks are adaptive: get harder after success, gently easier after struggle. Never show failure states — a miss is "Kailia needs a little more light, try again!" not "Wrong."
- Each difficulty level maps to an approximate developmental age band per domain. Store this mapping in a single config file (e.g. `lib/milestones.ts`) so it is easy to review and adjust with real data.
- Kids see only story rewards: map regions unlocked, companion creatures collected, badges. No scores, timers, or grades on the child-facing screens.
- Parents get a separate dashboard: per-domain developmental-age estimate, trend over time, and plain-language notes. Include a disclaimer that this is a screening/guidance tool, not a clinical diagnosis.
- **Where ages may appear (Kelvin, 2026-10-21):** developmental-age estimates are shown ONLY on the assessment results (`/results`) and the progress report (`/progress`), and always as a range ("about 2–3 yrs"), never a single age. Every other screen (world map, activity library, games, Parent Zone, adventure world) may name a stage such as "Little Explorer" but never an age. Gameplay never computes or changes an age; it only logs numbers.

## Developmental bands & the Parent & Baby Quest Library
For children below preschool developmental level, screens teach very little by themselves — so the app coaches the parent instead:
- `lib/difficulty.ts` maps assessment results to game difficulty tiers (tiny/small/big) AND to developmental bands: **Band 1 "Little Sprout"** (≈6–18 mo), **Band 2 "Little Explorer"** (≈18 mo–2½ y), **Band 3 "Little Adventurer"** (≈2½–4 y). Band null = school-age, world-map games carry the load.
- `lib/activities.ts` is the Parent & Baby Quest Library: 50+ real-world, OT-informed activities (by band × six domains), each framed as a Noel quest with steps, materials, and a "what to watch for" skill note.
- `/activities` shows 3 date-stable daily quests plus the browsable library; parents report each with one tap (Did it / Tried with help / Not yet) — reports persist per day and feed `lib/metrics.ts` for the future parent dashboard.
- The world map and results page surface a prominent library card whenever a band is detected.

## Daily care pet
- The child's companion friend is also a Tamagotchi-style pet. It sits beside Noel on the world map and pops up by itself once a day (never mid-game, never when another pop-up just showed) with ONE short task, rotating Feed (tracing) → Play (movement/calming) → Care (talking/routine). Then it rests until tomorrow.
- The pop-up is a "pet moment" in a floating box centered on every screen size (dimmed page behind, big X on the corner from `components/PopupClose.tsx`, tap outside or Escape also closes; rendered on `<body>` so nothing sits over it). Map pop-ups (Today's Quests, Starlight, land box) use the same X and `OnTop`. Inside, the pet drops onto its cushion, asks in a read-aloud speech bubble, and a growth path (egg → baby → little one → grown up, pictures only) sits on top. Feed = trace food on a plate, which then flies to the pet; Play/Care steps 4–6 are on-screen games set by each card's `game` in `lib/petTasks.ts` (breathe along, copy the pet's move and tap, or tick off steps); steps 1–3 show a grown-up card with one-tap report. Components live in `components/carepet/`.
- It hatches from an egg after the first day as the buddy picked at sign-up (or the parent's practice focus, same `focusCompanion` as Friends' adventures; Noel is the guide, never a buddy or pet) and grows baby → little one → grown up with each day played. It never gets sick, sad-hungry or dies; a missed day only gets "I missed you!".
- `lib/carepet/engine.ts` holds the rules with NO Kailia code (shared later with the older-adult app Pip): stages, the one-task-a-day scheduler, difficulty steps 1–6 (two easy wins up, one miss down, "with help" stays), free days. Keep it import-free; checks run with `node scripts/carepet.test.mjs`.
- `lib/kailiaPet.ts` is Kailia's adapter (consent-gated `kailia_pet_v1`, start step from the assessment band/tier, activities, metrics). Steps 1–3 use Parent & Baby Quest Library cards; steps 4–6 use `lib/petTasks.ts` (draft content for OT review).
- After 3 free days the daily task is part of the paid plan (only once `PAYWALL_ENABLED` is on in `lib/premium.ts`); the pet still visits happily and only a grown-up check leads to /upgrade.

## Rewards & the growing game library
- `lib/rewards.ts`: ✨ Starlight points (earned per quest, never framed as grades), Explorer level (sqrt curve), per-game highest-level, and companion creatures unlocked at starlight milestones — the hook for future real rewards. Key `kailia_rewards_v1` is consent-gated and registered in `CHILD_DATA_KEYS`.
- **Library strategy — engines × content × levels, not hand-built games.** The goal of 50+ games per age group per skill is reached multiplicatively: a few polished engines (swipe cards in `sky-mail`, tap-moving-targets in `firefly-catch`, walk-and-collect in `snack-quest`, tracing in `fine-motor`), each accepting new content decks (a card list = a "new game", no engine code) and infinite level scaling (speed/size/number curves per level). When adding variety, prefer a new content deck or level curve on an existing engine over a new engine; add a new engine only for a genuinely new interaction (e.g. pinch, rhythm tap, drag-to-sort).
- **Level ladder (`lib/levels.ts`, `components/LevelPath.tsx`).** Every leveled game shares one ladder: 30 levels in 6 chapters of 5 (Sprout → Sapling → Explorer → Ranger → Hero → Legend), each chapter mapped to an approximate age band (parent-facing only; review with a clinician), then endless ⭐ Star levels. Each game's intro shows the animated level map so a child can see progress and replay any level reached. A new game plugs in by keeping a `level` state, loading `nextGameLevel(id)`, rendering `<LevelPath>`, and scaling its own curve from `level`.
- **Age bands (Grok's review via Kelvin, 2026-11-25).** `lib/ageBands.ts` is the one place for how games behave at each age: together (under 3), little (3–4), early (5–6), big (7–8), older (9–12), each with tap size, speed, choices, path wiggle room, tap-to-move, timers, reading, math and memory pairs. A game reads them from `useGameFit(id).look` (the band follows the check-up for that game's skill, else the real age, moved a year by Family → Game level: Let the app adjust / Easier / Harder). Every game passes `roundEase(slips, items)` to `recordGameLevel`: 3 easy wins step up one extra level, 2 struggles quietly replay an easier one. Grown-ups can open any level under the level map (a sum first). Under 3 the map and every level map say "Play together". Family → Big Kid look sets `<html data-bigkid>` (fewer sparkles, smaller cast). `/review/ages` shows every game per band side by side. **Easy first, then a little challenging (Kelvin, 2026-11-25):** games whose level 1 is set by the band (Moonlight Match; Maze Runner under 7) keep that gentle first level, then `rampTo()` in `lib/gameCurves.ts` goes halfway on level 2 and reaches about what most children that age can do on level 3.
- **Age fit (Kelvin, 2026-11-25: "test each age group: too easy or too hard?").** Every leveled game is listed in `lib/ageFit.ts` (`FIT_GAMES`) with a `demand(level, tier)`: the age by which most children can do what that level asks, read off its real curve in `lib/gameCurves.ts` and the same norms as `lib/milestones.ts`. A new player starts on their own level (`hooks/useGameFit.ts`: `useGameFit(id)` gives the tier from the check-up estimate for that game's skill, plus `startLevel`; `openingLevel()` resumes where they left off). `node scripts/age-fit.test.mjs` checks children of 2–12 (`--markdown` prints the age-by-game table) and fails if any game starts an on-track child on a level that is too hard or climbs too fast in its first five levels. A new game: put its curve in `lib/gameCurves.ts`, add it to `FIT_GAMES`, use `useGameFit`, run the check. Jump links show it: `/go/game/<game>?age=8` (no `level`) opens where an 8-year-old starts. **Free part (Kelvin, 2026-11-25): the first 5 levels from the child's own start level** (plus every easier level below it), not levels 1–5: `isFreeLevel()` in `lib/ageFit.ts`.
- Games award starlight on completion (effort floor — wrong answers reduce but never zero it) and call `recordGameLevel` so progress persists.
- **Every round tells its own little story, and no two levels feel the same (Kelvin, 2026-11-23).** "First the dragons want berries, next they want to play with toys, next they're hungry for apples." Each game keeps a story deck (who needs help and why) and deals it with `lib/variety.ts` (`levelStories`, `levelStory`, `dealIndex`): every story is told before any comes back, never twice in a row, and the same level always tells the same stories. Numbers, characters, colors and twists must also move every level (not every 3 levels, not capped at level 6): see `lib/dragonBridgePlan.ts` for the model (story + sliding number window + twists + scene tint via `sceneLook`). A new game ships with its deck and level curve; new story sentences are recorded in Noel's (or the friend's) voice. **Whoever a story names must be on screen** (Kelvin, 2026-11-23: "it says the deer are safe, but it's only Noel"): each deck entry carries a `who`, drawn in the scene with `components/games/StoryGuest.tsx` and hopping when the round is won; a story with no `who` may only mention what is already drawn (Kailia, Noel, the friend, the scene).

## Plans & paywall

## Privacy & COPPA Rules (MANDATORY for every feature)
This app is child-directed (under 13), so children's privacy law (COPPA) applies to everything. Any new feature MUST follow these rules:

1. **Parent-first accounts.** Parents own the account (`lib/family.ts`, managed at `/family`). Child profiles hold the absolute minimum: first name/nickname, birth month+year, avatar emoji. NEVER add: last name, photos, email, phone, location, school, or free-text fields about the child.
2. **Children never enter personal info.** No child-facing screen may contain an input that could collect personal information. Name/age always come from the parent-created profile. Any new input field on a child-reachable screen needs explicit justification here.
3. **Data minimization.** Quest/game events log ONLY: child profile id, domain, task id, difficulty, accuracy, attempts, timing (`lib/metrics.ts` enforces the shape). No free text, no media, no device fingerprinting.
4. **Never record audio or video. Photos are allowed in exactly ONE narrow, safeguarded case — nowhere else.** Communication games use parent-assist tap ratings instead of voice recording; never request microphone access. The single photo exception is the **Printable Worksheets** feature (`lib/worksheets.ts`, `lib/photos.ts`, `/printables`): a parent photographs a completed paper tracing sheet so the app can mark it done and award starlight. Every use of that exception MUST keep all of these:
   - **Currently switched OFF for launch** (`PHOTO_VERIFY_ENABLED = false` in `lib/worksheets.ts`, decided 2026-10-19 to avoid needing a lawyer before launch): every worksheet uses the "We did it!" tap and no screen offers an upload. Turning it back on requires the lawyer review below first.
   - **On-device only, forever.** Photos live in IndexedDB (`lib/photos.ts`) on the family's own device and are NEVER uploaded, emailed, or sent to any server or third party. If cloud sync is ever added to this app, worksheet photos are explicitly excluded unless this rule is revisited with a lawyer.
   - **Its own separate consent**, distinct from the general child-data consent — `family.photoConsent` in `lib/family.ts` — shown the first time a parent tries to attach a photo, in plain language, before the file picker ever opens.
   - **Parent-initiated only.** The upload control is a `<input type="file">` the parent taps; the app never programmatically opens a camera or auto-captures anything, and no child-facing screen contains a camera control.
   - **Full deletion coverage.** The IndexedDB photo store is wiped by both `deleteChildAndData` and `deleteEverything` in `lib/family.ts` — treat any new photo-storing code path the same way you'd treat a new `CHILD_DATA_KEYS` entry, or deletion is incomplete (a compliance bug).
   - **No other feature may add a camera or photo capture** without going through this same checklist and updating this section first.
   - This feature is a bigger legal surface than the rest of the app (photos of children are a heavily regulated data category) — flag it for a privacy lawyer's review before any public launch, separately from the rest of the privacy review.
5. **No third-party analytics, ads, or trackers.** Do not add analytics SDKs, ad SDKs, pixels, or session-replay tools. Essential service providers only (hosting). The old Supabase upload was removed for this reason — child data currently never leaves the device.
6. **Consent before collection.** No child profile can exist before the parent actively consents (checkbox + confirm; timestamp, method and policy version saved in `lib/family.ts`). When payments arrive, upgrade the consent `method` to card verification.
7. **Parental rights.** `/family` must always offer: view all stored child data (`childDataInventory()`), delete child profile + all data, delete entire account. When adding a new localStorage key or (later) server table that holds child data, ADD IT to `CHILD_DATA_KEYS` in `lib/family.ts` and to the data inventory, or deletion becomes incomplete — this is a compliance bug, not a nice-to-have.
8. **Retention.** Child data lives only while the profile is active. Deletion is immediate on-device; any future server copies must purge within 30 days. Policy pages live at `/privacy` and `/retention` (both marked DRAFT until legal review).
9. **Security.** No child data in URLs or query strings. When cloud sync is added: per-family access control (RLS) from day one, TLS in transit, encryption at rest. See SECURITY.md.
10. **Wallet lab (ERC-8426 pet card) is an off-by-default experiment and the ONLY code path that sends anything to a server.** It lives behind `WALLET_LAB_ENABLED=1`, a grown-up check, on a test blockchain only, linked from no child screen. It sends pet data only (kind, stage, happy days, hellos) plus Apple device push tokens; never child data. Cards cannot be sold or traded and hold no value. Its device key `kailia_walletpet_v1` is in `CHILD_DATA_KEYS`, and `deleteChildAndData`/`deleteEverything` also delete the card on the server. Do not enable it for real families, add fields to the card, or move it to a real-money network without a privacy lawyer's review. Details: `docs/wallet-lab.md`.

## Tone & UX conventions
- Warm, encouraging narrator voice. Kailia speaks in short, simple sentences.
- Big touch targets, minimal text for pre-readers, audio narration for all instructions.
- Sessions are short: each land is completable in 3–5 minutes.
- Celebrate effort, not just correctness (animations, sounds, companion reactions).
- Accessible: works with touch and mouse, high-contrast friendly, no rapid flashing.

## Voice & sound
- **Noel reads every game aloud.** A game marks Noel's speech bubble and its intro story card with `data-read-aloud`; `components/NoelVoice.tsx` (mounted in `app/play/layout.tsx`) reads whatever appears or changes there, and a tap replays it. A game that speaks for itself through `say()` in `lib/voice.ts` leaves the attribute off; a listening game drops it while its sounds play (see Echo Song).
- **Recorded voices, made here, for free.** `scripts/voice-lines.mjs` gathers every fixed sentence into `voice/lines.json`; `scripts/record-voice.py` records them offline with the open-source Kokoro voice (Apache-2.0) into `public/voice/` + `manifest.json`. `lib/voice.ts` reads a line one sentence at a time: each recorded sentence plays in Noel's own voice, and only a sentence with no recording (the child's name, a changing sentence not yet listed) is read by the device, in a man's voice for Noel (Kelvin, 2026-11-25: one missing sentence used to send the whole bubble to the phone's girl voice, "a girl robot"). `node scripts/voice-miss-check.mjs <url>` plays every game at many levels on a phone-sized screen and lists Noel sentences with no recording into `voice/misses.json`; `voice-lines.mjs` folds them into `voice/lines.json`. Run it after adding game text, then record. Sentences with a changing word (a number, a friend's name, a shape, an animal) are listed once in `scripts/voice-dynamic.mjs` with the words that can fill them, and every version is recorded; keep one changing word per sentence so the list stays small. Kailia's short cheers (`lib/kailiaVoice.ts`, `voice/kailia-lines.json`, `--speaker kailia`) play now and then when she jumps; she stays quiet without recordings. After adding or changing game text, re-run both scripts. Names are said via `KAILIA_SOUND`/`NOEL_SOUND` in the script and `lib/nameSounds.ts` for the device voice: **Kailia = "KAI-LEE-yah" ("Kai" rhymes with sky, then "Lee-ya"); Noel = "No-EL" (two beats)** (Kelvin, 2026-11-20; never "kay-LEE-uh" or "Nole"). `node scripts/name-sounds.test.mjs` fails if either changes or a name line was recorded another way. Kailia has no recorded voice yet: Kelvin rejected the free ones as robotic, so she stays silent.
- **Pet calls.** Each friend has its own short call made in code by `scripts/pet-sounds.py` into `public/sounds/pets/<kind>.mp3`; `lib/petSounds.ts` plays it when a pet is tapped or cheers (at most every 5 s).
- **Music.** `lib/music.ts` is a soft generated loop in every game except listening games (`LISTENING_GAMES` in `components/GameMusic.tsx`); it dips while anyone talks. Keep it low and mellow: no long high tones.
- Privacy: all voices and sounds are made ahead of time and shipped as static files. The app never calls a voice service and never records the child.

## Showing Kelvin a change: jump links (EVERY thread, EVERY change)
Kelvin reviews on his phone. Never send him a link that starts at the front page, sign-up or Parent Zone and makes him click his way to the change. **Always send a jump link that opens straight on whatever you changed**, on the preview where the change lives.
- Jump links live at `/go/...` (`lib/jump.ts`, `app/go/`). Each one makes a test child "Robin" on the device with the right progress and opens the screen or pop-up directly. `/go` lists them all.
- Examples: `/go/pet-popup` (new egg), `/go/pet-popup?pet=grown`, `/go/pet-popup?pet=little&task=play` (task = feed/play/care), `/go/map`, `/go/map?all=1` (everything unlocked), `/go/land/dragons-cave` (a land's quest sheet), `/go/land-done`, `/go/new-friend`, `/go/daily-quests`, `/go/game/moonlight-match?level=3` (any game, any level; levels 6+ turn Plus on), `/go/friends`, `/go/friend/sage`, `/go/garden`, `/go/results`, `/go/progress`, `/go/family`, `/go/activities`, `/go/upgrade`, `/go/setup`, `/go/try`, `/go/home`. Extras on any link: `?age=3` (years), `?buddy=sage` (or fox/turtle/owl/unicorn/dragon/parrot), `?all=1`, `?plus=1|0`, `?calm=1|0` (calm mode on/off).
- Full link = preview address + jump path, e.g. `https://kailias-story-git-claude-project-01f98c-mikekelvin18s-projects.vercel.app/go/pet-popup`.
- **Off on the real public site**: `/go` answers "not found" unless the build is a Vercel preview, `next dev`, or `KAILIA_JUMP_LINKS=on` (`lib/jumpGate.ts`). Never set that variable for Production.
- **Outside reviewers (Grok, Muse, other AI tools) get `/review`**: a guide page with no sign-up that starts them via `/go/review` (finished check-up, everything open, Plus on, map with nothing popping up) and lists a jump link for every screen and game. Same preview-only gate. Add new screens and games there too.
- If you change something no jump link reaches yet (a new pop-up, a new game, a new page), add it to `planJump` and `JUMP_LINKS` in `lib/jump.ts` in the same change, then send that link.

## Engineering conventions
- **Robot checks look at the app like Kelvin does (Kelvin, 2026-11-26: "check EVERYTHING: game play, art, speed, voice").** `npm test` projects: `logic`, `computer` (tests/e2e: every game played to its party) and `phone` (tests/look, 390×844 upright and 844×390 sideways): framing (characters whole, on screen, not stacked, pet drawn fully in its bubble), sizes (things to count ≥22 px, answers to tap ≥24 px, numbers ≥14 px and inside their badge, nothing standing on an answer), map size, pictures of every screen and game compared with approved ones, speed, and Noel always in his recorded voice. A new game: mark Kailia/friends with `data-actor` (KailiaActor/Actor do it), countable things with `data-count-thing`, answers as buttons in `[data-play-field]` or `data-opt`. Approved pictures are taken on GitHub's machine: when a change is meant to change a look, put `[update pictures]` in the commit message and look at every retaken picture before merging.
- Build on the existing app and its current `/play` route — extend, don't rewrite, unless a rewrite is clearly justified and approved first.
- Keep game logic separate from measurement logic: games emit events; a scoring module interprets them.
- One reusable "quest engine" component; each mini-game is a plugin conforming to a shared interface (start, onEvent, complete → metrics payload).
- Write metrics to whatever persistence the app already uses; if none exists, propose the simplest option first.
- Ask before adding heavy dependencies. Prefer plain React/canvas/SVG for mini-games before reaching for a game engine.
